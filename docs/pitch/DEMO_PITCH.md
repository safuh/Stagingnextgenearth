# NextGen For Earth — Short Founder Demo Pitch

## 90-second opening

NextGen For Earth already has the mission, programmes, research activity and public presence. The next step is to connect those assets to the systems that coordinate delivery and demonstrate results.

We are building one organisational platform around that mission. The public website communicates the organisation's work and will include a dedicated Climate Intelligence hub for Kenya and East Africa. The hub will publish carefully reviewed climate indicators, regional trends and research insights with their sources, periods, methods and limitations visible. The workspace is where authorised teams manage people, programmes, learning, research, climate information, partners and evidence.

The research layer is designed to take a publication or study from discovery and source attribution through proposal, protocol and ethics review, datasets, climate evidence, analysis and publication. Climate intelligence is not limited to manual field submissions: the architecture brings together external sources such as NASA POWER and CHIRPS, with Copernicus ERA5 subject to deployment configuration, alongside reviewed field observations.

The learning layer connects four proposed curriculum tracks to course delivery, enrolment, assessment, mentorship, progress and certificates. These curricula are being developed for organisational review; the existing demo course shells should not be mistaken for approved final content.

The objective is practical: reduce fragmented records, make programme delivery easier to manage, give leadership a current operational view, and give partners and funders a clearer evidence trail.

## 7-minute demo sequence

1. **Public story (45 sec):** show the mission, programme pillars, research and published impact claims. Identify website-reported metrics as source claims, not audited results.
2. **Founder workspace (60 sec):** show organisation overview, people/roles, partner verification and audit controls.
3. **Research library (90 sec):** open a website-imported publication, show its source provenance, preview external scholarly metadata and create a new study in concept status.
4. **Research governance (60 sec):** show proposal/protocol/ethics gates, dataset submission/review and analysis provenance. Do not imply a concept is approved or active.
5. **Climate intelligence (75 sec):** open the redesigned public Climate Intelligence hub and methodology. If a genuine provider-backed release has been approved and published, inspect its data class, unit, geography, period, method, licence and limitations. Then enter the authenticated Climate Studio: run bounded CHIRPS ingestion, inspect the sample, record independent batch quality review, draft a versioned release and demonstrate the approval/publication queue. If the provider is not configured or no release exists, show the honest no-data state; never fabricate public values.
6. **Field-data loop (45 sec):** demonstrate the synthetic ODK simulator only when no live Central test endpoint is configured. Label all fixture records Synthetic demo and show duplicate suppression and pending review.
7. **Learning and programme delivery (60 sec):** show the proposed curriculum catalogue, course structure, enrolment/assessment workflow and programme activities. Clearly distinguish placeholder lessons from approved instructional content.
8. **Close (25 sec):** return to the leadership proposition: one connected system for research, youth development, climate stewardship, healthier communities and evidence.

## Closing statement

We are not proposing another disconnected website or spreadsheet. We are building the operating layer that connects the organisation's people, programmes, research, learning, partners and evidence—so that its mission can be delivered, governed and measured with greater clarity.

## Demo integrity checklist

- Use dedicated staging/demo accounts and synthetic learner identities.
- Label imported website records, external provider data and synthetic fixtures.
- Public climate visuals must use published releases only; a verified observation is not automatically public.
- Verify both a recent successful provider ingestion and an approved published public climate release before showing public climate values.
- Do not describe ODK simulator activity as a live Central connection.
- Do not present website-reported impact figures as independently audited.
- Do not present unverified partner prospects as signed partnerships.
- Do not present draft curriculum content as approved or accredited.
- Do not expose private records, credentials, storage keys or invitation links.


## Research-linked climate evidence

The public climate hub now includes a research-context layer. A CMS climate insight must reference both a published provider-backed climate release and a separately published research publication; it is not visible publicly until both are available. This lets the organisation connect climate context with its research portfolio while preserving provenance and avoiding unsupported causal claims.


## Learning capacity

The learning workspace now includes four pilot tracks across research methods, climate and health, youth climate stewardship and Indigenous/community knowledge. Twenty-four substantive lessons include applied practice, self-checks and official further reading, supported by four formative quizzes and learner progress. These materials are positioned as a subject-matter review pilot rather than accredited training.


## Climate evidence freshness

The public hub now shows the release publication date, provider retrieval completion and the data-period end separately. Public climate endpoints also support ETag/304 conditional reads, improving cache efficiency without hiding data age or calling historical data live.
