# NextGen for Earth — Founder Presentation

**From a public website to a connected climate-action operating platform**  
Prepared for founder and leadership review · 25 November 2026

## 1. The opportunity
NextGen for Earth already communicates a clear mission: training Africa’s next generation of climate scientists and translating research into community action. Its public website describes research capacity building, healthcare worker training, youth climate stewardship, Indigenous knowledge transfer, research publications, institutional relationships, fundraising priorities, field activity, team members and regional ambassadors. The opportunity is to connect that public story to the operational systems that deliver it.

## 2. What the current website tells us
The current public site presents four programme pillars; three recurring training tracks; a theory of change; 12 listed publications; an active climate-seasonality and ageing study in Busia and Kilifi; four funding propositions; safeguarding and PSEA commitments; nine named institutional/research partners; a field gallery; two videos; leadership, staff, mentors, ambassadors and mentees; testimonials; and contact/newsletter channels. Source: https://nextgenforearth.org/ (captured 25 November 2026).

## 3. The proposed digital platform
A single organisation workspace connected to a modern public-facing site. The public site explains the mission and publishes approved programmes, research, stories and opportunities. The workspace manages people and roles, partners, programmes, applications, training, learning progress, research records, climate data, field observations, CMS review and reporting.

## 4. Programme portfolio
- **Research Capacity Building:** weekly research methods, study design, systematic reviews, data collection and management, evidence synthesis, grant writing, ethics and safeguarding.
- **Healthcare Worker Training:** monthly climate-health, heat, extreme-weather, service-delivery, resilience and mental-health learning.
- **Youth Climate Stewardship:** climate literacy, environmental stewardship, tree planting/restoration, peer mentorship, community engagement and peer support.
- **Indigenous Knowledge Transfer:** preserve and amplify traditional ecological knowledge for adaptation and resilience.

## 5. Research and knowledge
The site lists 12 publications across climate and health, youth-inclusive climate research, maternal health, ageing, climate science and community engagement. The SENIORS study description focuses on climate seasonality and the health and wellbeing of older adults in Busia and Kilifi. The platform provides a structured place for study records, collaborators, protocols, ethics decisions, datasets, publications and climate observations.

## 6. People and partnerships
The public site names leadership, board/advisory members, mentors, employees, regional ambassadors and environmental mentees. The demo imports the published directory as source-attributed records, not as login accounts. Partner names are imported as website-listed relationships; verification remains pending until the organisation confirms the relationship and permissions.

## 7. Impact and accountability
The current website displays 100+ youth trained, 4,000+ children reached, 12 publications, 35+ youth mentored in systematic data synthesis and 40,000+ trees planted. These are **website-reported figures**, not independently audited platform metrics. The platform should preserve source, reporting period, methodology and evidence attachments for future validation.

## 8. What the platform adds
- One authoritative people directory with multiple roles and controlled membership access.
- Programme catalogue, projects, activities, milestones and indicators.
- Volunteer and employment applications, onboarding and safeguarding workflow.
- Course, module, lesson, assessment, attendance, mentorship and certificate management.
- Partner records, relationship owners, evidence and reporting.
- Research governance, publication registry and climate-intelligence integrations.
- Field data collection through ODK-compatible workflows.
- CMS drafts, review, approval, publication and audit history.
- Executive dashboards and exportable reports.

## 9. Demonstration journey
1. Public landing page: mission, programmes, research, impact, gallery and contact.
2. Founder workspace: organisation overview, governance, roles and audit history.
3. People and partners: inspect imported source records and their review states.
4. Programme operations: open a programme, create an activity and track a milestone.
5. Learning: course catalogue, learner application, approval, lesson progress and assessment.
6. Research and climate: study record, publication and climate-intelligence view.
7. Field data: ODK form discovery, synthetic demo submission and sync history.
8. CMS: edit a page, submit for review and publish with an independent reviewer.

## 10. Rollout proposal
**Phase A — Confirm and configure:** approve content inventory, roles, data ownership, safeguarding and staging environment.  
**Phase B — Import and validate:** load public website copy and media, reconcile people/partners/programmes, verify publication metadata and define impact indicators.  
**Phase C — Pilot:** train a small group of administrators, programme leads, researchers and learner representatives; execute acceptance tests.  
**Phase D — Launch:** production security review, backup/restore test, monitoring, DNS/HTTPS, staff onboarding and handover.

## 11. Decisions requested
- Nominate a content and data owner for sign-off.
- Confirm which team profiles and photographs may be reused.
- Confirm institutional partner relationships and approved logos.
- Confirm impact figures, periods and evidence.
- Provide staging infrastructure and DNS/HTTPS ownership.
- Approve a pilot cohort and acceptance criteria.

**Positioning:** The proposal is not to replace the organisation’s mission or public identity. It is to give that mission an operational platform: one connected place to coordinate people, programmes, research, learning, partners and evidence of impact.
