# NextGen For Earth — Initial Curriculum Blueprint

**Status:** Proposed curriculum templates for organisational review. These are instructional designs, not yet approved organisational policy or accredited qualifications.

## Design principles

- Use the four programme pillars already represented in the organisation's public materials.
- Build competency-based courses with observable outcomes, practical assignments and transparent assessment.
- Use Kenya/East Africa examples where suitable while avoiding claims that one community represents all communities.
- Treat Indigenous and local knowledge as knowledge held by rights-bearing communities. Obtain free, prior and informed consent where applicable; respect attribution, confidentiality, ownership and restrictions on recording or publishing knowledge.
- Make content accessible, low-bandwidth friendly and suitable for blended/online delivery.
- Adapt depth, language, duration and safeguarding to the age and prior experience of each cohort.
- Require organisational subject-matter review before publishing lessons or issuing certificates.

## Course 1 — Research Methods and Evidence Practice

**Audience:** Early-career researchers, research assistants and youth entering supervised research.  
**Suggested duration:** 8 weeks; 2–3 guided learning hours per week.  
**Prerequisites:** Basic reading, writing and digital literacy.  
**Completion:** All core lessons completed, research proposal assignment passed, ethics/data-management checks passed and final evidence synthesis submitted.

### Course learning outcomes

Learners will be able to:
1. Formulate a focused, answerable research question and justify its relevance.
2. Select a defensible study design and sampling approach.
3. Apply ethical, safeguarding and informed-consent principles.
4. Develop data collection instruments and manage data securely.
5. Assess source quality, bias, validity and limitations.
6. Conduct a structured literature search and synthesise evidence.
7. Communicate findings accurately for academic and non-academic audiences.
8. Prepare a basic research proposal and dissemination plan.

### Modules and lesson plans

**Module 1 — Research foundations and question development**
- Lesson 1: What research can and cannot establish — evidence, assumptions, description, association and causation.
- Lesson 2: From broad topic to researchable question — PICO/PEO and qualitative question frameworks.
- Lesson 3: Objectives, hypotheses and conceptual frameworks — alignment and feasibility.
- Practical: Turn a broad climate-health or youth-stewardship topic into one primary question and three objectives.

**Module 2 — Study design, sampling and field methods**
- Lesson 1: Qualitative, quantitative and mixed-methods designs — fit to question.
- Lesson 2: Sampling frames, sample size concepts, selection bias and representativeness.
- Lesson 3: Interviews, focus groups, surveys, observation and field protocols.
- Practical: Draft a sampling and recruitment plan; identify limitations and mitigation steps.

**Module 3 — Research ethics, safeguarding and data governance**
- Lesson 1: Informed consent, voluntary participation, withdrawal and participant dignity.
- Lesson 2: Research involving minors and vulnerable groups — safeguarding, assent, guardian consent and referral boundaries.
- Lesson 3: Confidentiality, data minimisation, access control, retention and secure storage.
- Practical: Prepare a participant information sheet, consent checklist and data-management plan for review. This is not a substitute for ethics committee approval.

**Module 4 — Data collection, quality and management**
- Lesson 1: Instrument design, piloting, translation and enumerator training.
- Lesson 2: Data dictionaries, identifiers, missing values, validation and field quality checks.
- Lesson 3: Secure data handling, versioning, provenance and reproducibility.
- Practical: Build a small synthetic dataset and data dictionary; document validation rules.

**Module 5 — Literature discovery and evidence synthesis**
- Lesson 1: Search strategies, databases, keywords and inclusion/exclusion criteria.
- Lesson 2: Screening, critical appraisal, source quality and risk of bias.
- Lesson 3: Evidence tables, thematic synthesis and systematic-review boundaries.
- Practical: Produce a reproducible search log and evidence matrix from public literature.

**Module 6 — Analysis, interpretation and limitations**
- Lesson 1: Descriptive statistics, distributions, uncertainty and appropriate visualisation.
- Lesson 2: Qualitative coding, themes, triangulation and reflexivity.
- Lesson 3: Missing data, confounding, overinterpretation and limits of causal inference.
- Practical: Interpret a supplied synthetic dataset and write a limitations statement.

**Module 7 — Research writing, grants and dissemination**
- Lesson 1: Scientific structure — abstract, introduction, methods, results and discussion.
- Lesson 2: Citation integrity, authorship, plagiarism, intellectual property and open research.
- Lesson 3: Policy briefs, community feedback, presentations and accessible communication.
- Practical: Draft a two-page proposal summary and a one-page community-facing brief.

**Module 8 — Capstone research proposal**
- Lesson 1: Integrating question, rationale, methods, ethics, timeline and budget.
- Lesson 2: Peer review, revision and response to reviewer comments.
- Lesson 3: Research pitch and dissemination planning.
- Capstone: Submit a proposal pack with question, objectives, design, sampling, instruments outline, ethics risks, data plan, timeline, budget assumptions and dissemination approach.

### Assessment plan

- Module checks: short knowledge quizzes (20%).
- Methods and sampling exercise (15%).
- Ethics and data-management pack (15%, must pass).
- Evidence synthesis matrix (20%).
- Final proposal/capstone (30%, rubric-scored).
- Ethics/safeguarding failure is a completion gate, not something offset by a high quiz score.

## Course 2 — Climate and Health Resilience

**Audience:** Healthcare workers, community health personnel and health programme staff.  
**Suggested duration:** 6 weeks; 2 guided hours per week plus a workplace reflection.  
**Prerequisites:** Basic health-service orientation. This course supports awareness and service resilience; it does not confer clinical credentials or replace clinical protocols.

### Course learning outcomes

Learners will be able to:
1. Explain key pathways linking climate hazards and health.
2. Recognise heat, flood, drought and extreme-weather risks to health services.
3. Interpret basic local climate information with uncertainty and source awareness.
4. Identify climate-sensitive service continuity risks.
5. Communicate practical prevention and referral information within their professional scope.
6. Develop a small facility/community resilience action plan.

### Modules and lesson plans

**Module 1 — Climate-health foundations**
- Lesson 1: Climate variability, climate change, hazards, exposure and vulnerability.
- Lesson 2: Direct and indirect health pathways — heat, air quality, water, food, vector-borne disease and mental health.
- Lesson 3: Equity, age, disability, occupation and social determinants of climate-health risk.
- Activity: Map climate-health pathways for one local service catchment.

**Module 2 — Heat and health**
- Lesson 1: Heat exposure, dehydration, heat exhaustion and heatstroke — recognition and escalation within clinical protocols.
- Lesson 2: Occupational heat, vulnerable groups and prevention communication.
- Lesson 3: Heat-health alerts, local thresholds and limits of generic thresholds.
- Activity: Review a synthetic heat-risk bulletin and prepare a facility communication.

**Module 3 — Floods, drought and extreme weather**
- Lesson 1: Flood-related injury, water contamination, displacement and service disruption.
- Lesson 2: Drought, food security, water access and nutrition pathways.
- Lesson 3: Extreme weather preparedness, continuity of care and referral networks.
- Activity: Complete a facility hazard and continuity checklist.

**Module 4 — Climate information for health decisions**
- Lesson 1: Weather observations, reanalysis, satellite estimates and climate projections — what each represents.
- Lesson 2: Reading rainfall and temperature series, anomalies, baselines and uncertainty.
- Lesson 3: Linking climate information to health surveillance without making unsupported causal claims.
- Activity: Interpret a sourced climate chart and document its limitations.

**Module 5 — Resilient and climate-responsive services**
- Lesson 1: Facility resilience, energy/water continuity and essential-service prioritisation.
- Lesson 2: Risk communication, community engagement and inclusive messaging.
- Lesson 3: Coordination with local government, disaster management and community structures.
- Activity: Draft a service-continuity improvement proposal.

**Module 6 — Mental health, reflection and action**
- Lesson 1: Climate-related distress, psychosocial support and professional boundaries.
- Lesson 2: Community listening, referral pathways and avoiding stigma.
- Lesson 3: Monitoring a small climate-health resilience action.
- Capstone: Present a facility/community resilience plan with risk, action, owner, indicator and review date.

### Assessment plan

- Knowledge checks (20%).
- Climate information interpretation (20%).
- Facility hazard/continuity assessment (25%).
- Resilience action plan and presentation (35%).

## Course 3 — Youth Climate Stewardship and Community Action

**Audience:** Youth participants, school/community climate clubs and youth volunteers.  
**Suggested duration:** 8 weeks; age-banded facilitation and safeguarding required.  
**Prerequisites:** None. Adapt reading level and activities for cohort age.

### Course learning outcomes

Learners will be able to:
1. Explain the greenhouse effect, climate impacts and local environmental connections.
2. Identify local environmental challenges using observation and credible evidence.
3. Plan safe, inclusive and community-informed stewardship activities.
4. Participate in restoration and tree-care activities using locally appropriate guidance.
5. Communicate climate information and counter misinformation.
6. Work in teams, reflect on leadership and document activities responsibly.

### Modules and lesson plans

**Module 1 — Climate science for everyday life**
- Lesson 1: Weather versus climate; greenhouse effect and human influence.
- Lesson 2: Climate impacts in East Africa — rainfall variability, heat, drought, floods and ecosystems.
- Lesson 3: Climate evidence, uncertainty and spotting misinformation.
- Activity: Create a local climate-learning poster with cited sources.

**Module 2 — Ecosystems and environmental stewardship**
- Lesson 1: Biodiversity, soils, water, forests, wetlands and ecosystem services.
- Lesson 2: Local environmental observation and simple citizen-science methods.
- Lesson 3: Waste, consumption, pollution and circular practices.
- Activity: Complete a supervised school/community environmental observation.

**Module 3 — Restoration and tree stewardship**
- Lesson 1: Native species, site suitability, community priorities and ecological risks.
- Lesson 2: Seedling handling, planting safety, aftercare and survival monitoring.
- Lesson 3: Restoration evidence — planted is not the same as surviving or restored.
- Activity: Prepare a supervised planting and aftercare plan with survival indicators.

**Module 4 — Community research and citizen science**
- Lesson 1: Asking a community question and observing responsibly.
- Lesson 2: Simple surveys, observation logs, consent and privacy.
- Lesson 3: Data quality, bias, safe photography and reporting.
- Activity: Produce a small, non-sensitive community observation log.

**Module 5 — Youth leadership and peer mentorship**
- Lesson 1: Inclusive leadership, teamwork and shared decision-making.
- Lesson 2: Peer mentoring, listening, boundaries and referral to trusted adults.
- Lesson 3: Planning a youth-led activity with roles and a realistic budget.
- Activity: Team charter and activity plan.

**Module 6 — Communication and advocacy**
- Lesson 1: Storytelling with evidence and respectful representation.
- Lesson 2: Public speaking, digital communication and misinformation.
- Lesson 3: Constructive engagement with schools, communities and decision-makers.
- Activity: Present a short evidence-based climate message.

**Module 7 — Community action project**
- Lesson 1: Community needs assessment and stakeholder mapping.
- Lesson 2: Design, risk assessment, safeguarding and permissions.
- Lesson 3: Activity delivery, documentation and reflection.
- Activity: Deliver or simulate one approved community action.

**Module 8 — Reflection and impact evidence**
- Lesson 1: Outputs, outcomes and long-term impact — what can be claimed.
- Lesson 2: Attendance, participation, feedback and safe evidence capture.
- Lesson 3: Reflection, next steps and peer presentation.
- Capstone: Present a team action project with evidence, learning, limitations and maintenance plan.

### Assessment plan

- Knowledge and misinformation checks (15%).
- Environmental observation log (15%).
- Team action plan and safeguarding checklist (20%).
- Supervised action/project evidence (30%).
- Reflection and presentation (20%).

Participation by minors requires approved safeguarding procedures, age-appropriate assent, guardian consent where required, safe supervision and restricted handling of identifiable data and images.

## Course 4 — Indigenous and Community Knowledge for Climate Resilience

**Audience:** Community facilitators, researchers, youth leaders and partner organisations working with local knowledge holders.  
**Suggested duration:** 6 weeks; co-designed with participating communities.  
**Prerequisites:** Respectful facilitation and willingness to follow community protocols.

### Course learning outcomes

Learners will be able to:
1. Explain the value and diversity of Indigenous and local knowledge systems.
2. Identify appropriate community-led approaches to knowledge exchange.
3. Apply consent, attribution, ownership and benefit-sharing principles.
4. Document only knowledge that communities agree may be recorded and shared.
5. Bring knowledge systems into dialogue with scientific evidence without extracting or subordinating either.
6. Co-design a community-defined climate resilience activity and governance plan.

### Modules and lesson plans

**Module 1 — Knowledge systems, place and context**
- Lesson 1: Indigenous knowledge, local knowledge and situated practice.
- Lesson 2: Land, water, biodiversity, livelihoods, culture and intergenerational learning.
- Lesson 3: Power, representation, historical extraction and researcher positionality.
- Activity: Stakeholder and knowledge-governance map approved by the community.

**Module 2 — Ethical and respectful engagement**
- Lesson 1: Free, prior and informed consent and the right to decline.
- Lesson 2: Collective and individual permissions, attribution and confidentiality.
- Lesson 3: Data sovereignty, intellectual property, restricted knowledge and benefit sharing.
- Activity: Co-design a community permission and knowledge-use protocol; obtain local review.

**Module 3 — Participatory methods**
- Lesson 1: Listening circles, participatory mapping and community timelines.
- Lesson 2: Intergenerational dialogue and inclusive facilitation.
- Lesson 3: Translation, interpretation, accessibility and safe documentation.
- Activity: Facilitate a simulated listening session without collecting sensitive knowledge.

**Module 4 — Climate adaptation and resilience**
- Lesson 1: Community-defined climate risks, priorities and existing coping strategies.
- Lesson 2: Ecosystem stewardship, livelihoods, food/water security and adaptation options.
- Lesson 3: Assessing trade-offs, unintended harm and distribution of benefits.
- Activity: Co-develop an adaptation options matrix for community validation.

**Module 5 — Knowledge dialogue and evidence**
- Lesson 1: Respectful dialogue between community knowledge and scientific evidence.
- Lesson 2: Triangulation, disagreement, uncertainty and context.
- Lesson 3: What may be shared publicly, what remains restricted and who decides.
- Activity: Prepare a community-reviewed synthesis with explicit permissions and limitations.

**Module 6 — Community-led action and accountability**
- Lesson 1: Co-designing an action with community governance and decision rights.
- Lesson 2: Benefit sharing, feedback, grievance pathways and accountability.
- Lesson 3: Monitoring community-defined outcomes and returning results.
- Capstone: Submit a community-approved resilience concept note and knowledge-governance plan.

### Assessment plan

- Knowledge and ethics checks (20%).
- Engagement protocol and consent plan (25%, must pass).
- Participatory adaptation options matrix (20%).
- Community-reviewed capstone (35%, must pass community permission and governance checks).

## Shared assessment and certificate rules

- Use a 100-point rubric with explicit criteria and performance descriptors.
- Require a minimum 70% overall score plus all mandatory safeguarding/ethics gates.
- Permit reasonable reassessment and accessible alternatives.
- Record assessor, rubric version, feedback, submission version and decision timestamp.
- Certificates state the course title, learning hours, completion date and issuing organisation; they must not imply professional licensure or external accreditation.
- Certificate verification exposes only minimal validity information and supports revocation.

## Content production checklist

Before publishing any course:
- [ ] Appoint a subject-matter lead and independent reviewer.
- [ ] Confirm audience, language, reading level and delivery constraints.
- [ ] Replace every placeholder with approved lesson content and accessible resources.
- [ ] Confirm source citations, image permissions and reuse rights.
- [ ] Validate outcomes, lesson alignment, quiz answer keys and rubrics.
- [ ] Complete safeguarding, privacy, accessibility and cultural review.
- [ ] Pilot with a small cohort and revise using feedback.
- [ ] Approve course version and record publication decision.


## Implemented curriculum pilot content (M121)

The seed catalogue now contains four pilot tracks aligned to the organisation's programme areas:

1. Research Methods and Evidence Practice
2. Climate and Health Resilience
3. Youth Climate Stewardship and Community Action
4. Indigenous and Community Knowledge for Climate Resilience

Each track contains two modules and six core lessons (24 core lessons total); the research and healthcare tracks each include one additional applied lesson, bringing the pilot to 26 lessons. Lesson bodies include a learning outcome, core concepts, applied practice and a self-check. Four formative quizzes provide four questions per track with a 70% pass mark. Lessons link to official source material from WHO, IPCC, UNICEF, FAO, UNESCO and the United Nations. The seed operation updates existing demo lessons and creates or updates the formative quiz records idempotently.

The learner classroom now presents the course overview, module/lesson counts, estimated guided minutes, module-level completion and readable lesson-content panels.

**Release boundary:** these are substantive pilot learning materials prepared for organisational subject-matter review. They are not accredited qualifications, clinical protocols, ethics approval or substitutes for locally approved safeguarding procedures. Community knowledge modules explicitly require community authority and permission; restricted knowledge must not be collected or published by default. The pilot content must be reviewed by the appropriate research, clinical, safeguarding and community leads before formal cohort delivery.

The updated seed file is `scripts/data/nextgen_curriculum_content.json`. The Render API container startup invokes the seed script for the default demo organisation `next-gen-for-earth-demo`, so the staging deployment refreshes that catalogue automatically. For a different organisation slug, an authorised operator must run `python scripts/seed_nextgen_demo.py --organization-slug <organisation-slug>` in the deployed application environment.
